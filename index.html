About

Research with depth. Collaboration with reach.

I am a Lecturer in AI and Machine Learning at the University of Kent and a Visiting Fellow in the Department of Computer Science and Technology at the University of Cambridge. I hold a PhD in Computer Science from Durham University and previously conducted research at Cambridge and Oxford. My work connects principled machine learning with applications in healthcare, finance and education.

My aim is to develop AI that not only performs well, but whose representations, reasoning and decisions can be understood and relied upon.

Research agenda

Four connected directions

These themes share a common goal: discovering what makes intelligent systems robust, interpretable and dependable as tasks and environments change.

01 · Reliability

Trustworthy, Reliable & Agentic AI

AI safety, reliable reasoning, model monitoring, uncertainty, long-horizon behaviour and autonomous and multi-agent systems.

02 · Reasoning

Structured & Neuro-Symbolic AI

Graph logic, knowledge-grounded models, structured memory, compositional reasoning and knowledge graphs.

03 · Representation

Geometric & Topological Learning

Geometry and topology of neural representations, graphs, hypergraphs, sheaves, causal structure and learning dynamics.

04 · Generalisation

Causal & Reliable Learning

Causal representation learning, distribution shift, robustness and reliable AI for healthcare, financial systems and education.

Join & collaborate

Bring an ambitious question. Build meaningful research.

I welcome enquiries from motivated PhD applicants, research students, postdoctoral researchers and academic or industry collaborators. Particularly relevant interests include reliable agents, representation dynamics, structured reasoning, graph and geometric learning, and high-stakes AI.

Strong collaborations start with a concrete research question, relevant literature, a feasible first experiment and complementary expertise. External PhD co-supervision and research visits can be discussed where an appropriate host and arrangements exist.

To get in touch: share a short CV, your research interests, a specific question you would like to investigate, and relevant projects or code.
